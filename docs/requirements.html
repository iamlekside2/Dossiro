Context

The problem being solved

Nigerian enterprises, banks, hospitals and public agencies hold their records in shared drives, email and paper. They are increasingly required — by regulators, by auditors, by the Nigeria Data Protection Act, and by their own boards — to control who can see a record and to show afterwards who did.

The established document management products in this market are Windows-era software: a Windows-only server, a desktop administration tool, a Java desktop client, per-seat licences priced for a different economy, and a deployment that needs a partner on site. They work, and they are what buyers compare against, but they impose an IT burden most Nigerian organisations do not want and a cost most cannot justify.

Dossiro's opportunity is the gap between the compliance requirement and the delivery model. The compliance posture is not negotiable and must be matched. The delivery model — install, maintain, administer from a desktop, one database at a time — is where an incumbent can be beaten outright.

Strategy

The product bets

Five decisions that the requirements below assume. They are stated here so that a requirement derived from them is not later questioned in isolation.

1. Browser-first, with no desktop component at all

Including administration. Incumbent products administer from a Windows desktop tool; matching that would concede the single clearest advantage available.

2. Multi-tenant SaaS as the default delivery

One deployment serving many organisations, each at its own address. Self-hosting is offered where a regulator demands it, not as the standard path.

3. A customer configures their own document types

Without a developer. This is the backbone of the category: advanced search, retention rules, workflow triggers and automatic indexing all key off it. A product without it is a file store with permissions.

4. Capability is not divided into separate purchasable modules

Workflow, retention, notifications, indexing and search are one product. The incumbent pattern of selling each as an add-on is a pricing habit, not a customer need, and undercutting it is straightforwardly valuable.

5. Text extraction and AI assistance are core, not an upsell

Every document that enters is made searchable. Assistance that proposes a name, a folder and a classification is part of filing, not a premium tier. The proposal is always confirmed by a person before anything is renamed or moved.

Sequence

Release phases

Phases follow dependency, not preference. Document types come first because a quarter of the product keys off them, and text extraction second because capture, search and automatic indexing all wait on it.

PhaseContainsReady when
1 · Sellable All P0 requirements. Tenancy, filing, access, document types, capture with text extraction, search, sharing, the audit trail, retention and legal hold, browser administration, provisioning and licensing. A customer can be provisioned, file their records, control access, share safely, and satisfy an auditor — with nothing installed.
2 · Competitive P1. Workflow and notifications, signatures, annotation and redaction, offline and mobile, automatic indexing, reports, seat pooling, SOC 2. Dossiro wins a head-to-head evaluation against an incumbent on capability, not only on price and delivery.
3 · Extending P2. Forms, co-editing, semantic search, public portal, external lookups, configuration portability, certificate signing. The remaining gaps against the category are closed and the product leads on the areas the incumbents have abandoned.

Unresolved

Open questions

These need answers from the business, not from engineering.

  1. Pricing shape. Per seat, per tenancy, or per volume of records? PLT-6 and PLT-9 cannot be specified precisely until this is settled.
  2. Reserved and pooled seats — is the complexity worth it? It matches what buyers know, and pooling is the mechanism that makes an occasional user affordable. It is also the incumbent's most disliked behaviour. Offering only one simple model may be the stronger position.
  3. Which regulated sector is targeted first? Banking, health and public sector each add requirements — health in particular adds a standard we have not scoped.
  4. Hosting location. NFR-5 requires Nigerian residency on request. Whether that is the default for all customers is a cost and latency decision.
  5. Is self-hosting offered at launch or withheld? It widens the addressable market and multiplies the support burden.
  6. Migration from what? Most buyers hold records in shared drives and in an incumbent system. Whether import tooling is a product feature or a service offering changes Phase 1.
