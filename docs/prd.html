Definition

What Dossiro is

A web application where an organisation files its records, controls who may see each one, shares individual documents outside the company under conditions it sets, and can afterwards prove exactly who did what.

Every customer organisation is a tenant: its own users, its own filing structure, its own records, reachable at its own web address. One deployment serves many tenants and nothing crosses between them.

The distinguishing claim is not storage — storage is commodity. It is that the record of activity is evidence. Every consequential action is written to an append-only, hash-chained trail that the database itself refuses to alter, and the chain can be recomputed on demand to prove it has not been touched. An organisation that must answer to an auditor, a regulator or a court can show its working.

Origin

Why it exists

Calm Global evaluated a commercial document management product as a reseller. The evaluation established two things: there is real demand among Nigerian enterprises and public bodies for compliant document management, and the available products are desktop-era software sold at licence costs that assume a different market.

Dossiro is the response: a browser-first system built for organisations that need the compliance posture without the desktop deployment, priced and hosted for the market it serves. It is designed to be sold as a managed service, with self-hosting available for buyers whose regulator requires it.

Structure

The three realms

Operating the platform and using the product are different jobs, so they are different places.

1. The operator console

Where Calm Global administers the business: provisioning tenants, suspending them for non-payment, issuing licences. Suspending or closing a tenant locks a whole company out of its own records, so it requires a written reason, and that reason is recorded in both the platform's audit trail and the tenant's own, where nobody can later edit it.

2. The tenant workbench

Where the customer's staff work: the filing cabinet, search, sharing, and the audit trail. This is the product as most people experience it, and the bulk of this document describes it.

3. Tenant administration

Where a customer administers themselves: people, roles, departments, branches, web addresses. Currently a section of the workbench; splitting it into its own area is an agreed next step.

A deliberate separation

Calm Global appears twice: once as the operator, and once as an ordinary tenant holding its own records. Those are separate organisations with separate data. The operator console cannot read the records tenancy, and vice versa.

Core model

How access is decided

Two independent systems both have to say yes: permissions, which come from a person's role and say what kind of thing they may do at all, and grants, which are attached to folders and documents and say which specific records they may touch.

Levels

A grant carries one level, and the levels are ordered: NONE → READ → DOWNLOAD → WRITE → APPROVE → MANAGE → OWNER. DOWNLOAD is deliberately separate from READ: being allowed to look at a contract on screen is not the same as being allowed to take a copy away.

Resolution

A grant can be made to a person, a department, or a role, and can be attached to a document or to a folder, where it flows down to everything beneath. When several apply, the most specific scope wins — a rule on the document beats one on its folder, which beats one on the folder above. At equal specificity, deny beats allow.

The case that proves it

A clerk in Accounts Payable inherits write access to the whole Finance cabinet through their department, but carries an explicit deny on the Invoices subfolder. They can file into Finance and are refused on Invoices. This is asserted on every verification run.

Classification

Independently of who you are, every document carries a classification — PUBLIC, INTERNAL, CONFIDENTIAL or RESTRICTED — and the classification constrains what may be done with it regardless of permission. A RESTRICTED record cannot be shared outside the organisation at all; a CONFIDENTIAL one cannot be shared as an open link with no named recipient, and only a manager or administrator may share it externally.

A folder that is marked as not inheriting access is sealed: grants from above do not reach into it, and only explicit grants apply. This is how an HR cabinet stays closed to an administrator who can otherwise see everything.

The differentiator

Evidence and the audit trail

Every consequential action writes an entry. Each entry hashes its own content together with the hash of the entry before it, so the log forms a chain: change or remove any entry and every subsequent hash stops agreeing. GET /api/audit/integrity recomputes the chain and reports the first entry that disagrees.

The guarantee does not rest on application code being well behaved. A database trigger rejects UPDATE and DELETE on the audit table outright, so no role — including the owner, and including anyone with direct database access through the application's own credentials — can quietly edit history. Both refusals are asserted on every verification run.

Writes are serialised per organisation with an advisory lock, so two simultaneous actions cannot chain off the same predecessor and fork the history.

Known limit, stated plainly

The hashed content covers the action, the actor and the resource. It does not yet cover the originating address, the resource name, or the metadata payload. Those fields cannot be edited in practice — the trigger blocks it — but they are not cryptographically bound. Widening the hash would invalidate every existing entry, so this is a decision to take before real customer data lands.

External access

Sharing outside the organisation

A share link lets someone without an account reach exactly one document. The person issuing it sets the terms: an expiry, an optional access code, a download cap, whether downloading is permitted at all, whether the document is watermarked, and which email addresses may open it.

Opening a protected link is two steps. The recipient first authorises with the access code and receives a short-lived signed ticket; the bytes are only served against that ticket. A ticket issued for one link does not work on another. View-only is enforced at the point the bytes are served, not merely hidden in the interface: requesting the same document as an attachment is refused.

Revocation is immediate — a revoked link returns gone on the next request. Every open is written to the audit trail.

No ORM, deliberately

Every query is written by hand as parameterised SQL. The access-control resolution alone involves recursive folder walks, department hierarchies and specificity ordering; expressing that through an ORM's abstraction obscured what the database was actually being asked to do. Row types are generated from the live schema, so a column that changes shape breaks the build rather than failing at runtime.

Three details worth knowing

  • Transactions propagate implicitly. A nested transaction joins the one already running rather than deadlocking against it, so a service can be called standalone or inside a larger operation without knowing which.
  • Timestamps are UTC end to end. The Postgres driver reads and writes timestamps in the machine's local time by default, which silently shifts every date by the server's offset. This is corrected in three places at once: the type parser, parameter serialisation, and the session timezone.
  • Request origin is ambient. The address and user agent are carried in request-scoped context, so an audit entry records where an action came from without each call site remembering to pass it.

Schema

The data model

49 tables. The ones that matter most to understanding the system:

TableHolds
organizationsTenants, plus the single platform organisation that runs the service
usersPeople. Email is unique per organisation, not globally, so a consultant can belong to two tenants.
foldersThe cabinet tree, with a materialised path for subtree queries
documentsRecords, each pointing at a current version
document_versionsImmutable history. A new edition never overwrites the previous one.
document_indexExtracted text for full-text search, separate from the document row
access_grantsWho may reach what, at which level, allow or deny
share_linksExternal links and their conditions
audit_eventsThe hash chain. Append-only, enforced by trigger.
tenant_hostnamesWhich web address belongs to which tenant

Identifiers are generated in the application rather than by the database, so a row's id is known before it is written — which is what lets a folder's materialised path be correct in the insert itself rather than needing a follow-up update.

Migrations

Plain SQL files applied by a small runner that records a checksum of each. A migration edited after it has been applied is detected and refused, rather than silently diverging between environments.

Commercial

Licensing

Licences are Ed25519-signed and verified offline, so a self-hosted deployment with no outbound connectivity still enforces its terms. Seat entitlement is read from the signature, never from a column in the database — editing organizations.seatLimit directly changes nothing, which is asserted on every verification run.

The largest gap

User-defined document types

Customers expect to define their own kinds of document — a Contract, a Personnel File — each with its own typed, indexed fields they can search on. The schema does not express this yet, and the HR area is blocked behind it. It is the clearest functional gap against established products in this market.

Delivery

Deployment

Two models, the same software.

Managed. The default. A customer gets their own tenancy on Calm Global's platform at their own web address. Nothing to install, nothing to maintain.

Self-hosted. For buyers whose regulator requires records to stay on their own infrastructure. Licences verify offline precisely so this works without outbound connectivity.

A tenant's web address is what identifies them at sign-in. Visiting a tenant's own address settles which organisation is being signed into before anyone types anything; the shared sign-in asks only when the address settles nothing and the person belongs to more than one organisation.

Outstanding

Open decisions

Storage backend

Whether to add S3 as a storage driver. This is genuinely two separate questions that are easy to conflate: using object storage as the backend for document bytes, and exporting documents to a customer's own bucket as a published deliverable. The second must be classification-aware — publishing a restricted record to a bucket the organisation does not control would defeat the entire access model.

The audit hash canonical

Whether to widen the hashed content to cover the originating address and metadata. Doing so invalidates every existing entry, so it should be settled before real data lands.

Tenant administration split

Agreed in principle: move tenant self-administration out of the workbench into its own area. Not started.

Preview and editing

The document preview and editing panes are still the designer's illustration. A real preview needs the content stream and a viewer; editing is the buy-not-build decision recorded above.
